## Context

Over ten years, separate directorates had commissioned their own systems for closely related work. The result was fourteen applications with overlapping data, three hosting arrangements and no shared view of a case from start to finish.

Leadership needed to reduce run cost and procurement exposure, but every system supported a live public service. Disruption was not an option.

## Approach

We began with a four-week assessment: an inventory of every application, its users, data and contracts, scored against business criticality and technical condition. That produced a target architecture of five core platforms and a migration sequence the institution's governance board could approve in one sitting.

Delivery ran in four release windows. Each window moved one group of services, with parallel running and a rehearsed rollback plan. Our engineers worked inside the institution's own teams, so knowledge stayed in-house when we stepped back.

## Outcome

- Five core platforms replaced fourteen applications.
- Annual run cost fell by 38% in the first full year.
- No critical incidents during cutover; citizen-facing services stayed available throughout.
